Exact mask, conditional etch

The supplied clock-gate footprint — all ten polygons, exactly as printed — is projected through its 30 µm mask with deterministic ion and radical transport, then coupled to the Belen SF₆/O₂ silicon surface equations. Everything measured is fixed; everything unmeasured is a slider you can move.

TL;DR

  • Your 30 µm printed mask is a radical filter, not an ion filter. Through the exact clock-gate layout, ions arrive ~92% intact; thermal radicals only ~7% (direct line of sight). Every deep, enclosed part of the pattern is fluorine-starved.
  • So the etch depth is set by how much F and O survive bouncing off your polymer walls — which nobody has measured. Everything else in this page is computed from your geometry and published chemistry; that one quantity is the amber slider.
  • Outcome at the central scenario: ~2.8 µm mean depth with a 2.5–3.0 µm spread across the layout, shallowest in the radical-starved zones. A conditional interval, not a certified forecast of your tool.
  • What we need from you to make it a forecast: (1) one blanket Si etch on your tool with the same recipe, (2) the achieved DC self-bias, (3) pre/post polymer mask height. One sacrificial run covers all three.

What you are looking at

  • The mask is Arun’s exact printed clock-gate layout — all ten polygons, 30 µm tall, sitting on a thin silicon film. Nothing is redrawn or idealised.
  • Ions come straight down and mostly clear the mask: about 92% of the beam reaches the floor at 1.5° spread.
  • Thermal radicals come from every direction and are mostly blocked: only about 7% reach the floor directly. The deep mask is a radical filter, not an ion filter — that is the central finding.
  • The depth pattern is chemistry, not a fit. Each point’s local ion, F and O fluxes feed the coupled Belen SF₆/O₂ silicon equations; the open-feature rate is pinned to Miao’s independently measured 3.5–3.9 µm at two minutes.
  • One thing is unmeasured: how much F and O survive bouncing off the polymer walls. That is the amber slider, and it is what moves the depth interval. A single blanket-etch measurement on Arun’s tool pins it.

Outcome at the central scenario: the layout etches to about 2.80 µm mean depth with a 2.53–2.95 µm spread across the pattern; the radical-starved corners and narrow tracks etch least.
