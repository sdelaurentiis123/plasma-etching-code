**Fine-structure-resolved electron collisions with atomic chlorine**

- **Citation:** Y. Wang, O. Zatsarinny, K. Bartschat, and J.-P. Booth,
  “Fine-structure-resolved electron collisions from chlorine atoms in the
  `(3p5) 2P3/2` and `(3p5) 2P1/2` states,” *Physical Review A* **87**,
  022703 (2013).
- **DOI:** `10.1103/PhysRevA.87.022703`
- **Official article:**
  `https://journals.aps.org/pra/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevA.87.022703`

## Claims table

| # | verified source claim | use and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| W1 | The `Cl(2P1/2)` spin-orbit state is `0.109 eV` above the `Cl(2P3/2)` ground state. | Physical fine-structure gap. It rejects Kemaneci/COMSOL's use of the absolute `1.35 eV` Figure-10 coordinate as a transition energy. |
| W2 | At electron-temperature equilibrium, the excited-state population can approach half the ground-state population. | Consistent with statistical weights `g=2J+1`: `g_upper/g_lower=2/4`. A reverse rate with unit degeneracy cannot recover this limit. |
| W3 | The paper calculates both excitation and superelastic de-excitation cross sections and reports the state-changing results as substantially more stable across target models than the low-energy elastic results. | Supports a future evaluated fine-structure pair. It does not validate the Kemaneci Maxwellian fit numerically because the paper's machine-readable arrays are not archived here. |
| W4 | Low-energy atomic elastic predictions differ substantially across calculations near the Ramsauer minimum, and the paper reports no experimental collision data that resolve the disagreement. | Atomic elastic transport is a model-form uncertainty, not measured closure. The official COMSOL `Cl_mom_xsec` table may be replayed but not promoted to evaluated physical authority. |

## Use decision

Use `0.109 eV` and the explicit `J=3/2` and `J=1/2` statistical weights for
physical fine-structure detailed balance. Do not digitize Figure 2 while the
authors' underlying arrays may be recoverable; request the data or locate an
official archive first. Until then, the evaluated atomic excitation provider
remains fail-closed rather than inheriting COMSOL's `1.35 eV` gap.
