## Claims table

| # | verified source claim | use and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Z1 | In 50% Cl2/50% Kr at 400 W and 60 mTorr, no-window PAE is 235 nm/min; opaque, quartz, and MgF2 windows give 11.4 +/-0.9, 13.5 +/-0.8, and 19.4 +/-2.3 nm/min. | Quantitative wavelength discriminator; window insertion also changes local plasma density, so it is not an absolute spectral yield. |
| Z2 | Quartz transmits roughly above 170 nm and MgF2 above roughly 120 nm; the authors infer about 90% of PAE is driven below 120 nm and about 10% above. | Forbids applying Du's 105-nm yield to a 139-nm chlorine line. |
| Z3 | The paper cites analogous photoetch yields near 0--10 atoms/photon at 130--135 nm versus around 100 near 112--128 nm. | A nonpredictive longwave sensitivity ceiling only; target/material pairs differ. |
| Z4 | Across halogen mixtures the PAE rate tracks surface halogen coverage times an optical-emission proxy. | Motivates a spectrum x surface-state response, not a photon-only additive constant. |

## Use decision

The production model must resolve wavelength bins. A scalar “VUV flux” cannot
close depth because shortwave and 135--139 nm photons have radically different
surface leverage.
