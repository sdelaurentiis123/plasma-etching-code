A Study on the Etching Characteristics of High Aspect
Ratio Oxide Etching using C4F6 Plasma in Inductively
Coupled Plasma with Low Frequency Bias Power
Name:             Byungjun Woo

Department:       Control and Instrumentation Engineering

Major:            Measurement & Sensor

Thesis Advisor: Kwang-Ho Kwon



In this thesis, a study on high aspect ratio (HAR) oxide etching


processes in inductively coupled plasma (ICP) using C4F6 gas and


low-frequency (2 MHz) bias power was conducted. Amid growing


efforts to reduce carbon emissions in the semiconductor industry,


C4F6 is environmentally friendly with a Global Warming Potential


(GWP) of less than 1. Thus, to verify the feasibility of using C4F6


for HAR oxide etching, we investigated the etching characteristics


of CF4 + X + He mixed gas (X = C4F6) on Amorphous Carbon

                                 i
Layer (ACL) and Silicon Oxide (SiO2), including etch rates, etch


selectivity, and etch profiles of the X gas. Optical Emission


Spectroscopy (OES) and a dual Langmuir probe were employed


for plasma diagnostics. Compared to conventional C4F8 mixed


plasma, the C4F6 mixed plasma demonstrated similar SiO2/ACL


selectivity and superior etch profiles. The chemical changes on the


etched ACL and SiO2 surfaces were analyzed using X-ray


Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS), supporting the above results


by showing increased passivation due to the rise in CFX species.


Additionally, experiments were conducted to study the correlation


of etching characteristics with variations in source power and bias


power. Plasma diagnostics revealed that as the source power


increased, the ion density and electron temperature contributing to


plasma generation also increased. With an increase in bias power,


the value of -Vdc associated with ion bombardment energy



                                ii
increased. Furthermore, the etch profiles derived from these


parameters highlighted the etching characteristics corresponding to


each variable. These etching results indicate that the use of C4F6


gas can significantly contribute to the development of next-


generation HAR oxide etching processes.

                                                                  Introduction




1.1 Current trends in the semiconductor industry




                Figure.1.1 Semiconductor Potential roadmap extension



Currently, the semiconductor industry is reaching the limits of miniaturization with


the advancement of microfabrication technologies for high performance, low power


consumption, and miniaturization. This has led to increased interest in alternative

                                       -1-
technologies such as advanced packaging. Enhancing chip performance and


production efficiency through advanced processes for pre-processing and post-


processing, especially achieving integration below 3nm and 3D integration, is a


primary focus. Therefore, the development of microprocessors for detailed circuit


implementation is essential. In particular, the transition from 2D planar structures to


3D vertical stacking structures in NAND architecture has made the formation of


nanoscale patterns in semiconductor devices dependent on five key processes:


deposition, lithography, etching, ion implantation, and planarization. Among these


processes, etching is gaining more attention in semiconductor manufacturing


because it is repeatedly applied alongside deposition and lithography in increasingly


complex structures. If the desired area is not etched cleanly during etching,


misalignment can occur during multi-patterning processes due to uneven etch


profiles, referred to as misalignment caused by rough etch surfaces. [1] During


etching, the ion incident angle is influenced by the mask shape, making it difficult to


obtain vertical profiles, and issues such as necking and bowing occur during High-



                                        -2-
Aspect-Ratio (HAR) etching processes. [2] Therefore, optimizing the etching


process through appropriate control of etching gases is essential to adjust the mask


shape and ensure the formation of vertical profiles.




          Figure 1.2. Estimated share of etching process in global market




                                        -3-
1.2 Dry-etching technology




Etching can be divided into dry etching and wet etching. Wet etching involves the


use of reactive solutions to selectively remove materials. However, wet etching


results in isotropic etching, where the etch rate is high and uniform in both vertical


and horizontal directions. Additionally, the pattern size achievable is limited, making


it impractical for smaller sizes. On the other hand, plasma etching selectively


removes materials using reactive gases, vapors, or ions generated by plasma. In


current semiconductor processes, as the component sizes decrease, and circuits


become more complex, anisotropic etching in dry etching processes is essential.


Plasma dry etching overcomes the issue of isotropy and provides higher patterning


resolution. However, due to the complexity of gas reactions and etching mechanisms


within the plasma, as well as various other parameters, modeling and controlling


plasma etching can be challenging.




                                        -4-
1.2.1 isotropic etching and anisotropic etching




When etching progresses at the same rate in both the lateral (x-direction) and


downward (y-direction) directions, it is called isotropic etching, resulting in etching


similar to that shown in Figure 1-(a). Conversely, when the etching rate in the y-


direction is greater than that in the x-direction, it is called anisotropic etching, as


shown in Figure 1-(b). In particular, when the etching rate in the x-direction is zero,


as depicted in Figure 1-(c), it represents an extreme case of anisotropic etching


known as vertical etching. Directional etching, as shown in Figure 1-(c), occurs when


the etching rate in the x-direction is lower than the etching rate in the y-direction,


and this type of etching is generally achievable in plasma etching.




                Figure 1.3 isotropic etching and anisotropic etching

                                        -5-
Figure 1.4. Plasma etching schematic diagram in the process of plasma dry etching,




In plasma dry etching processes, reactive gases are introduced into a vacuum


chamber, and a strong electric field is applied by a power source. This accelerates


electrons, which then collide with neutral atoms, leading to ionization,


recombination, dissociation, abstraction, and fragmentation processes. Ultimately,


plasma refers to ionized gas. When energy such as electricity is applied to gas



                                      -6-
composed of atoms and molecules, plasma is formed, consisting of electrons, ions,


dissociated gases, and photons, and it becomes electrically neutral. The etching


species in the plasma then diffuse towards the target surface, where they are adsorbed.


Due to the voltage applied to the electrode, ions gain directionality, and their impact


weakens the molecular bonds. The etching species adsorbed at the weakened bond


sites react more easily with the target material, forming volatile compounds. These


compounds then desorb from the surface, diffuse into the gas phase, and are


eventually evacuated by vacuum equipment. Dry etching technology utilizes


chemical reactions between surface materials and the impacts of ions to facilitate


precise pattern transfer for nanoscale patterning processes. [3]




A representative material for silicon insulators is silicon dioxide (SiO2). To etch


insulators, it is necessary to use etching reactive gases that have good reactivity with


each material and form volatile by-products. In today's semiconductor


manufacturing industry, gases with a high proportion of fluorine (F) such as CF4,



                                         -7-
C2F6, C4F6, and C4F8, which induce dry etching on the substrate surface due to their


high reactivity with F, are commonly used. [4-9] Additionally, various studies focus


on adding oxygen and adjusting the gas ratio to control the fluorocarbon (FC)


polymer layer, achieving anisotropic etch profiles through appropriate masking and


sidewall passivation.




                                       -8-
1.3 Multiple-patterning technology




For decades, miniaturization has been a major trend in the electronics market, leading


to the integration of more functions into single products and technological


advancements. Achieving these required improvements in existing lithography,


which led to the adoption of multiple patterning technology. Multiple patterning


includes overlaying smaller and denser patterns onto patterns engraved over a wide


area. Double patterning methods are broadly classified into SADP (Self-Aligned


Double Patterning) and LELE (Litho-Etch-Litho-Etch), which are extensively used


in the NAND flash and RAM memory manufacturing industries [10,11]. Among


them, one of the most common double patterning techniques is the LELE method,


which alternates lithography and etching steps twice. Particularly, the flexibility in


design is greatly enhanced as each mask can be independently optimized. In this


process, a perfect photomask pattern is divided into two low-density patterns. The


first pattern is transferred to the underlying hard mask layer by lithography after



                                        -9-
etching, and this process is repeated for the second pattern. The key to this process


is that the two patterns must be perfectly aligned. Imperfect alignment causes the


combined patterns to have significant relative displacements, leading to yield issues.




              Figure 1.5 LELE and SADP process schematic diagram.

1.4 3D NAND Flash memory HAR etching process technology




                   Fig. 1.6 V-NAND flash memory architecture




For example, in the production process of 3D NAND Flash, alternating layers of


oxide and nitride thin films are deposited, followed by the deposition of a hard mask


and the formation of openings to enable the etching of vertical channels. In this


process, it is essential to form holes penetrating the NAND uniformly and


consistently from the first layer to the last layer. Particularly, as recent 3D NAND


reaches the 2xx layer range, an increase in the number of layers requires high aspect

                                       - 11 -
ratio etching, leading to ongoing research on various methods and equipment


development for hole etching. In HARC etching processes, increasing production


costs and cycle times are occurring due to long process times. To overcome this


situation, methods to increase the selectivity of etching processes are being


researched. [12-16] One of these methods is to increase the amount of gas forming


polymers to increase passivation. However, excessive polymer formation can lead to


etch stoppage. For example, problems encountered during high aspect ratio etching


include hole blockage at the top of the pattern, uneven hole sizes at the top of the


pattern when etching down to the bottom (necking and bowing), pattern tilting, and


pattern closure. [17-26] Due to the challenges of these processes, meticulous control


of each process variable is necessary.

1.5 Capacitively Coupled Plasma (CCP) and Inductively Coupled

Plasma (ICP)




If we were to describe the plasma sources of CCP type and ICP type, CCP type injects


neutral gas particles between two anode and cathode electrodes and applies voltage


to generate plasma. On the other hand, ICP type generates plasma by applying


voltage using a circular coil at the top of the chamber. In CCP type, a high voltage


between the lower electrode and the plasma results in high ion energy, allowing


operation at high pressures. It also offers flexibility in operating RF frequencies and


provides excellent plasma uniformity, enabling large-area processes. However, at


low pressures, the gas pressure decreases, causing significant electron losses and


resulting in a relatively lower plasma density of around 109/cm3 compared to ICP's


1011/cm3. Additionally, the high ion energy in CCP type may lead to substrate damage


during processing.




                                        - 14 -
       ICP type, however, generates plasma by applying voltage using a circular coil


at the top of the chamber. It creates high-density magnetic fields even at low


pressures, resulting in a plasma density 10-100 times higher than that of CCP.


Moreover, it offers independent control over plasma density and sheath voltage,


providing flexibility to adjust individual factors as required for different processes.


Additionally, ICP operates at lower pressures, increasing the mean free path and


allowing for higher ion bombardment energy compared to CCP. [27,28]




            Figure 1.8 Schematic diagram of ICP type and CCP type equipment

1.6 Plasma system used in HAR etching process




The High Aspect Ratio (HAR) etching process has traditionally been performed


exclusively using Reactive Ion Etching (RIE) equipment. However, in previous HAR


etching processes using Capacitively Coupled Plasma (CCP) or RIE systems, there


was a tendency for the etch rate to sharply decrease as the aspect ratio increased.


This was due to the inability of ion flux and neutral radicals to reach the bottom of


the target material at high aspect ratios [29,30]. Additionally, issues related to profile


distortion have been reported as the aspect ratio increases in HAR etching processes


[31-34]. Various studies have been conducted to investigate the causes of such profile


distortion, which include changes in process gases and pressure, RF power and


frequency, and etching profile variations [6,35,36]. The results of these studies


suggest a correlation between ion energy, etch rate, and profile distortion due to the


adsorbed polymer layer. [17-26]




                                         - 16 -
In today's semiconductor process development, there are numerous studies and


challenges related to various profile distortions. However, the most critical condition


is to avoid profile distortion caused by ion energy by adjusting the ratio of carbon


(C) and fluorine (F) in CF gases for mask and sidewall passivation. Generally, in CF


gases, carbon is known to affect the FC polymer, while fluorine determines the


effectiveness of the etching. [37] To achieve higher aspect ratio etching, it is


necessary to individually control the source power, which contributes to plasma


density generation, and the bias power, which affects ion energy. In Inductively


Coupled Plasma (ICP), each element can be controlled individually, and it is known


to have advantages in etching processes with smaller patterns due to the benefits of


the mean free path at low pressure. [38]

1.7 Perfluorocarbon (PFC)- based gas




Perfluorocarbon (PFC)-based gases are the most widely used gases in semiconductor


and display manufacturing processes for etching and cleaning, consisting of


compounds of carbon and fluorine. [39] These PFC gases are known to have a high


Global Warming Potential (GWP) of over 8000, adversely affecting the ozone layer.


[39] Representative PFC gases include CF4, C2F6, C4F8, SF6, CHF3, and NF3. [39]


Especially, with the increase in patterning process steps due to Double Patterning

                                     - 18 -
Technology (DPT) and Quadruple Patterning Technology (QPT), the usage of PFC


gases is expected to continue rising. [40-42] The emission of PFC gases has been


steadily increasing over the past decade in tandem with advancements in


semiconductor processing technology. [43]

1.8 Importance of environmental issues




The Earth's average temperature has generally fluctuated between 1.0°C to -0.5°C.


From the 15th to the 19th century, temperatures were relatively low, but since the


20th century, a distinct upward trend has emerged. Unlike past temperature patterns


in the Earth's climate system, modern temperature increases are primarily attributed


to greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions. The rapid increase in GHG emissions due to


industrialization has accelerated temperature rise. Consequently, the Earth's climate

phenomenon formally identified in the 1972 report by the Club of Rome.


Subsequently, various countries have begun implementing policies and regulations

1.9 Research objectives




Plasma etching is an essential process step for manufacturing micro and nano


electronic devices. High-density plasmas such as helicon, inductively coupled


plasma (ICP), and electron cyclotron resonance (ECR) are known to offer many


advantages. Given the characteristics of ultra-precision semiconductors, the


manufacturing process varies depending on the product specifications. Any changes


in the existing process gases and conditions can lead to critical defects and yield loss.


Therefore, numerous evaluation procedures and verifications are required to reflect


the changed conditions in actual production processes. However, the lack of relevant

                                         - 23 -
research and systematic studies on gas processes and chemical properties poses


difficulties in applying them to actual etching processes. Continuous and sustained


research and interest in specific fields are essential for the development of etching


processes. Moreover, efforts to reduce carbon emissions in the continuously evolving


semiconductor industry should be considered alongside technological advancements.




       Figure 1.13. Chemical bonding structure of Hexafluoro-1,3-butadiene


C4F6 stands out as a promising candidate for overcoming environmental concerns


due to its low Global Warming Potential (GWP) index (<1) [56-58]. While this gas


is suitable for etching processes due to its low GWP, there are limited cases of its


application in etching processes, and no research has been conducted on high aspect



                                       - 24 -
ratio oxide etching using C4F6 in ICP systems. To confirm the potential of C4F6 as a


replacement for conventional etching gases, optimization of plasma etching


processes using appropriate operating gases and input process conditions is necessary.


Additionally, various experiments and measurements are required to determine the


critical process quality factors in semiconductor processes. Therefore, this study


investigated the etching mechanism and characteristics of C4F6 gas and evaluated its


potential application in high aspect ratio etching processes compared to conventional


PFC gases.

                                       - 28 -

2.1.4 RF supply device


The power supply unit is used to supply RF (13.56MHz, 2MHz) power for plasma

impedance matching circuit, supplies RF power through the upper coil to deliver

                                        - 29 -
energy to the plasma. An impedance matching circuit is installed between the power

independently of the equipment to maintain a constant temperature of the circulating

    2.2 Langmuir probe (Single , Double)




                            Figure 2.3 Double Langmuir probe




    The single Langmuir probe is a device used for measuring plasma parameters.


It can measure electron density, ion density, electron temperature, ion saturation


current, and electron energy distribution function. This equipment consists of a metal


probe, a variable power supply to apply voltage to the probe, and a current


measurement tool to measure the current flowing through the probe. The Langmuir


probe inserts a metal probe into the plasma and applies voltage to it to extract electron


or ion currents from the plasma for analysis. The basic information obtained is the I-

                                         - 31 -
V curve, which represents the relationship between the applied voltage and the


current. Analyzing this curve provides information such as ion saturation current


density, plasma density, electron temperature, plasma potential, floating potential,


and electron energy distribution function. When the probe voltage is increased above


the plasma potential (Vp), only electrons enter the probe. The current entering the


probe at this point is due to electrons, and in this region, the electron current should


remain constant regardless of voltage. However, in reality, the current tends to


increase with increasing voltage due to the increase in the effective area of the probe


caused by the increase in the sheath area around the probe. When the probe voltage


is decreased below Vp, electrons entering the probe must have higher energy than the


plasma potential. Electrons with lower energy are reflected by the probe. Therefore,


if the electron energy distribution follows a Boltzmann distribution, the electron


current in this region appears as an exponential function. Additionally, at certain


voltages, the current flowing through the probe becomes zero, which is referred to


as the floating potential. If Vp is decreased further below Vf, the electron current



                                        - 32 -
decreases significantly, and the ion current increases. In this region, known as the


ion saturation current, the current magnitude does not change significantly even with


decreasing voltage. By analyzing the characteristic curve of the Langmuir probe,


plasma parameters can be extracted, and the electron energy distribution function


can be obtained through calculations, including second derivatives. The single


Langmuir probe is a versatile device for measuring various plasma parameters,


especially the electron energy distribution function. Due to its advantages, the single


Langmuir probe has been actively used in understanding plasma generation


mechanisms, and measurement and interpretation methods continue to evolve. Care


should be taken in RF plasmas, as the plasma potential oscillating at RF frequencies


can cause distortion in the I-V characteristic curves.




                                        - 33 -
           Figure 2.4. I-V characteristic curve of Single Langmuir probe




The double Langmuir probe method is a device that can measure ion density, electron


temperature, and ion saturation current. It consists of two metal probes, a variable


power supply to apply voltage, and a device to measure the current flowing through


the probes. The characteristic curve of the double Langmuir probe also shows the


I-V characteristic curve representing the current variation with voltage. The


characteristic curve of the double Langmuir probe exhibits an origin-symmetrical


shape and has two advantages over the single Langmuir probe. With an electrically


isolated structure, unlike the single Langmuir probe, the measurement circuit is



                                      - 34 -
electrically insulated, resulting in less distortion of the I-V characteristic curve due


to changes in plasma potential. It can also be used in environments where the plasma


potential is not clearly known, such as when the internal surfaces of the chamber are


electrically insulated, as in semiconductor and display processes. The current


flowing through the double Langmuir probe has the same maximum size as the ion


saturation current, so measuring with a small current captures less perturbation to the


plasma discharge state. This feature makes it suitable for measurements in process


equipment where harsh environments are created by high-power RF power.

2.3 Optical Emission Spectroscopy (OES)




                     Figure 2.6. Optical emission spectroscopy




Optical emission spectroscopy is a method used to analyze elements by measuring


the specific wavelengths of light emitted due to changes in the electronic states of


the elements. This method has the advantage of being able to measure without


passing through the chamber, thus not affecting the plasma generated inside the


chamber. Through the analysis of specific wavelengths, it identifies the elements


present in the plasma and estimates the quantity of elements by varying the intensity


of light. However, this method has limitations in that it cannot measure plasma


species that do not emit light. Additionally, since the emission intensity of each




                                       - 36 -
plasma species is not directly proportional to its density, it is difficult to directly


compare the densities of different plasma species.




                               A + e → A* + e                               (1)

                            AB + e → A* + B + e                             (2)


Intense light is emitted from excited species through ion collision processes.




                           A + e (+ M) → A* (+ M)                            (3)




As the excited species A* transitions back to its ground state (A), light corresponding


to the energy difference is emitted.




                                  A* → A + h                                (7)




Optical emission spectroscopy is generated by electron collision and dissociation


processes in plasma. Light is emitted from excited states during ion detachment


                                        - 37 -
processes. Here, the excited state A* represents the emitting species, while e(+ M)


denotes neutral species, negative ions, electrons, third bodies, or surfaces in contact


with the plasma. To perform optical spectroscopy, appropriate optical systems and


detectors are required to measure the emitted light from the plasma. Light generated


in the plasma is transmitted to a spectrometer, passes through a diffraction grating,


and is spectrally measured. At the exit of the spectrometer, only light of specific


wavelengths is transmitted to the detector, generating a photocurrent proportional to


its intensity. For plasma species emitting at specific wavelengths, identification can


be achieved through the wavelength of the emitted light. However, since each plasma


exhibits a variety of spectral lines, identifying a specific plasma can be challenging.


For this reason, optical spectroscopy provides useful information mainly on the light


emitted by single or binary particles, as their spectra are simpler and narrower


compared to multi-atomic species. Additionally, due to variations in light intensity


depending on the plasma state, quantitative interpretation can be challenging.




                                        - 38 -
    2.4 X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS)




           Figure 2.7 X-Ray photoelectron analysis schematic diagram




    X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) is particularly useful for acquiring


information about very thin surface layers within 100 angstroms. It is often known


as Electron Spectroscopy for Chemical Analysis (ESCA). When X-rays (photons)


with specific energy interact with the sample surface, photoelectrons are emitted


from the surface atoms. By measuring the kinetic energy of these photons, we can


determine the binding energy (BE) of the inner electrons bound to the atomic nucleus,


which is the energy required to emit the internal electrons bound to the nucleus of



                                      - 39 -
the atom. This allows us to identify the surface components and understand their


chemical bonding states. The binding energy, characteristic of the atoms emitting the


photoelectrons, plays a crucial role in identifying the components. Additionally,


qualitative analysis of the sample can be performed through binding energy, and the


bonding states of atoms can be investigated through changes in binding energy. The


binding energy of photoelectrons is useful for identifying elements and evaluating


the properties of samples.

2.5 Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM)




Figure 2.8 Overview of Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM) Analysis schematic


                                  diagram




                                  - 41 -
           Figure 2.9 Function of Scanning Electron Microscope (SEM)




    Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) is primarily used to observe the fine


structure and morphology of solid samples, thanks to its capability to easily visualize


features in three-dimensional (3-D) images. It allows for the high-resolution


observation of complex surface structures and crystal forms in three dimensions. The


instrument consists of a condenser lens, an objective lens, an electron gun that


generates electron beams, a focusing lens that concentrates electron beams, and


injection coils that control the path of electrons exiting the electron gun filament.



                                        - 42 -
The electron beam is focused on the surface of the conductive sample to form a


concentrated electron beam area, which is then observed using electromagnetic


lenses. This method forms images through scanning and observation. Various


secondary emissions occur during this process, but the image is displayed to the


observer using secondary electrons generated from the interaction between the


sample and the electron beam. At this point, the brightness of the image is


proportional to the number of secondary electrons generated by the interaction


between the electron beam and the sample. Since the sample is monitored through


electrons, distortion may occur due to collisions of electrons if air is present


internally. Therefore, observations should be made under vacuum conditions (10- 5


Torr) to prevent this.

5. Conclusion

In this study, plasma etching characteristics and plasma diagnostics were conducted


by varying three parameters: gas ratio, Source power, and Bias power in a CF4 +


C4F6 + He mixed plasma. With the increasing complexity of circuit structures and


the repetition of processes in multiple-patterning techniques, it has been reported that


the flattening of etching profiles on both mask and pattern bottom surfaces is crucial


to prevent misalignment during patterning. [109,110] Additionally, due to the


complexity of circuits, efforts have been made to reduce carbon emissions and


explore new materials and gases. [111] C4F6 is an environmentally friendly gas with


a GWP index of less than 1 and a lifetime of 2 days. Recent research on high aspect


ratio etching using C4F6 gas and C4F8 gas has regained attention. However, previous


studies only focused on basic etching evaluation and plasma diagnostics using these


gases. [56-58] There were no studies specifically on high aspect ratio etching.


Furthermore, it has been reported that research on high-density plasma of ICP type,


as opposed to the conventional CCP type, is needed. [27-28] For high aspect ratio



                                        - 81 -
etching, research on process optimization for conventional gases is necessary in


addition to exploring new variables. We investigated the potential applicability of


C4F6 as an environmentally friendly alternative gas for high aspect ratio oxide


etching and examined the etching characteristics and plasma properties observed


when using C4F6 plasma with various parameter variations. Additionally, the most


vertical profiles were observed under the condition of CF4 / C4F6 / He (15/25/100,


sccm). In this study, by finely adjusting each parameter, we proposed the possibility


of HAR etching through process optimization.
