VERIFIED EXTRACT — report pp. 5-6

The report constructs the thermal incident radical flux as [CF] v / 4 and
the deposition flux from the measured deposition rate and polymer density.
Their ratio gives a CF sticking coefficient of 0.4.  Repeating the procedure
for CF2 gives a range of 0.01 to 0.07.

The report states that film deposition and film removal balance to create a
thin steady-state fluorocarbon film through which substrate etching occurs.
For the silicon datasets, etch rate alone scatters with film thickness because
ion density changes across conditions.  After normalizing by incident ion
number, the measured silicon etch yield decreases exponentially with FC-film
thickness across widely different conditions.

For the source's clean-Si condition, the reported yield is approximately
1.5 Si atoms per incident ion.  This is a condition-specific anchor, not a
universal ceiling for other materials, ions, or chemistries.

Monte Carlo stopping calculations are used to interpret the first 1-2 nm of
FC film as the major ion-energy-loss region.  Above roughly 1 nm, direct
ion energy deposition into Si becomes unimportant.  The report states that
roughly 2 nm of steady-state FC film produces SiO2 etch stop.

CLAIM BOUNDARY
- The CF and CF2 sticking coefficients belong to the documented discharge,
  analysis, and polymer-density convention.
- The exponential relation is an empirical measured topology; this extract
  does not digitize or invent its decay length.
- The approximately 1.5 Si/ion point is not transferable as a universal yield
  bound.
