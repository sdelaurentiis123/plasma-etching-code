Extraction method and boundary
------------------------------
The 15-page PDF was extracted with pdftotext -layout and the scientific claims
below were checked against original-resolution 300–600 dpi Poppler renders.
Pages 1301, 1309, 1310, 1311, and 1312 and Figures 7–10 were visually checked.
This is a concise, paraphrased, grep-ready extraction rather than a
redistribution of the full article. Equations and numerical values are retained
where they define the measurement or its validity boundary.

Scope and physical mechanism (pp. 1301–1303)
--------------------------------------------
Low-pressure O2 and Cl2 processing plasmas create O and Cl atoms primarily by
electron-impact dissociation. The atoms are lost primarily by heterogeneous
recombination at reactor walls and substrates, so the wall state controls
plasma composition and processing rates.

The article emphasizes that clean-surface beam coefficients are not generally
reactor coefficients. Positive ions, electrons, UV/VUV radiation, chamber
erosion, and deposited process material continuously condition the wall.
Bare-surface Cl recombination probabilities near 300 K had been reported as
0.2 on anodized aluminum and 0.7 on stainless steel, while values inferred in
operating Cl2 reactors were commonly 0.01–0.04.

Spinning-wall measurement (pp. 1302–1305, 1308–1310)
----------------------------------------------------
A substrate moves periodically between an inductively coupled plasma and a
differentially pumped analysis chamber. Surface and desorbing species are
measured by Auger electron spectroscopy or line-of-sight mass spectrometry.
Rotation frequency changes the reaction time, allowing prompt and delayed
Langmuir–Hinshelwood contributions to be separated.

For chlorine, Cl2 adsorption/desorption is present even with the plasma off.
The plasma-on pressure rise is corrected by the measured plasma-off response:

  ΔP_rec = ΔP_on - (n_Cl2,on / n_Cl2,off) ΔP_off

The corrected pressure rise is converted to net Cl2 desorption D_rec. The
reported Cl-atom recombination probability is

  gamma_Cl = 6 D_rec(tr -> 0) / Gamma_Cl

where Gamma_Cl is the incident Cl-atom flux and the desorption rate is
extrapolated to zero reaction time.

Conditioned-wall composition (pp. 1308–1309)
--------------------------------------------
After Cl2 plasma exposure the test surfaces acquire a silicon-oxychloride-rich
layer due to silica discharge-tube erosion. Reported conditioned compositions
were approximately Fe:Si:O:Cl = 1:13:13:3 on stainless steel and
Al:Si:O:Cl = 3:3:8:1 on anodized aluminum. The stainless surface had more
silicon and chlorine and a smoother morphology.

Figure 8 direct wall evidence (pp. 1309–1310)
---------------------------------------------
Figure 8 reports gamma_Cl versus n_Cl/n_Cl2 for plasma-conditioned stainless
steel (filled markers) and anodized aluminum (open markers). Marker shapes
identify pressures of 20, 10, 5, and 1.25 mTorr; the experimental ICP power
range is 100–600 W. Individual powers are not encoded for the plotted points.
The dashed curves are guides to the eye only.

For conditioned stainless steel, the text reports gamma_Cl from 0.004 to 0.03,
increasing with the Cl/Cl2 density ratio. Conditioned anodized aluminum follows
the same trend at values about a factor of two larger. These coefficients are
far below bare-surface values and are consistent with silica-rich conditioning.

The authors attribute the ratio dependence to competition for adsorption
sites. Increasing Cl2 can block sites needed for Cl adsorption and delayed
Langmuir–Hinshelwood recombination. The article explicitly warns that
n_Cl/n_Cl2 is not sufficient by itself: absolute Cl2 or O2 impingement density
also affects recombination dynamics.

Large-volume reactor inversion (pp. 1310–1311)
-----------------------------------------------
A 190 MHz surface-wave source feeds a 28 cm diameter stainless processing
chamber through a 14.6 cm fused-silica tube. Downstream electron density and
temperature are stated as 1e9–1e11 cm^-3 and 2–4 eV. The measured gas
temperature was near room temperature. Cl2 dissociation was measured by
plasma-sampling mass spectrometry and Xe actinometry.

The isothermal fluid model tracks electrons, Cl, Cl2, Cl+, Cl2+, and Cl- and
uses the Lee–Lieberman reaction set. Figure 9 compares measured dissociation
with curves for gamma_Cl = 0.01, 0.015, 0.02, 0.025, and 0.03.

The paper states that none of the constant-gamma curves adequately fits the
full pressure range. The gamma required to match the measurement increases
with pressure. This is consistent with the spinning-wall dependence and
forbids selecting one chamber-independent gamma from Figure 9.

The review reports cross-check values of gamma_Cl = 0.04 near
n_Cl/n_Cl2 ~ 30 from Malyshev et al., 0.02 near ratio ~3 from Corr et al., and
0.007 near ratio ~0.08 from Richards and Sawin.

Quartz-tube inversion (pp. 1311–1313)
-------------------------------------
A separate 2.45 GHz surface-wave plasma in a 6 mm inner-diameter fused-silica
tube was studied at 50 mTorr and 60 W absorbed power. Electron density,
dissociation, and electron temperature were measured along the tube.

The inferred gamma_Cl spans approximately 0.02–0.04, including about 0.042 at
z = 1 cm and 0.026 at z = 13 cm. These values exceed the <0.005 reported for
smooth annealed quartz exposed to an atomic beam. The article attributes the
reactor values to erosion, roughening, and a conditioned SiOxCly surface.
The small axial variation may be within experimental/fitting uncertainty and
may also contain a wall-temperature contribution.

Executable interpretation
-------------------------
1. Do not use a universal scalar gamma_Cl.
2. Carry wall conditioning/material, n_Cl/n_Cl2, absolute impingement density,
   pressure, and temperature as state or applicability information.
3. Figure 8 supplies a measured validation envelope, not a coefficient-
   selection target for feature depth.
4. Figure 9 supplies a strong negative gate: a model with one constant gamma
   must not claim pressure-wide predictive closure.
5. Recover the original Figure-8 papers (Guha et al., JAP 103, 013306 (2008),
   and Stafford et al., J. Phys. D 42, 055206 (2009)) before promoting an
   interpolation of the review figure to a production boundary provider.
